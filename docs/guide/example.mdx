# Example

```bash
cd example
flutter run -d chrome
```

The demo ships four props:

- `example/sprites/car/slices.png` — 15 slices, a red Formula 1 car with driver helmet, halo, open wings and number 1 (voxel-modelled by `make_volume.py`).
- `example/sprites/helicopter/` — 24 slices, an animated AH-64 military attack helicopter with spinning 4-blade main and tail rotors, ordnance, chin cannon and landing skids (`make_frames.py`).
- `example/sprites/spaceship/slices.png` — 21 slices, a Star Wars inspired starfighter.
- `example/sprites/house/slices.png` — 29 slices, a cottage with porch, picket fence and lawn (voxel-modelled by `make_volume.py`).
- `example/sprites/tree/slices.png` — 43 slices, a cherry-blossom tree carved with `--round`.
- `example/sprites/palmeira/slices.png` — 26 slices, a tropical palm tree.
- `example/assets/robot_stack.png` — 24 slices, generated by `tool/generate_demo_asset.py`.

It has sliders for yaw, pitch, scale and spacing, plus toggles for auto-spin,
wind sway (`StackMotion`), the 1 px outline and the ground shadow.

The **Helicopter demo** button in the app bar showcases real-time rotor animation
(`SpriteStackAnimation`) with a live Rotor RPM slider, take-off / hover altitude
effects, and landing pad graphics. It is phone-first: the stage auto-fits the
projected bounds and the control sheet is compact and scrollable.

## 3D scene demos

Three buttons in the app bar show the scene layer:

| Button | Screen | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orbit scene (3D camera) | `OrbitSceneScreen` | Drag-to-orbit diorama: house, car, tree and palm over the painted ground, with the sky gradient |
| Chase camera (follow with lag) | `ChaseCameraScreen` | A car lapping a stadium-shaped track (two straights, two turns) with a live culling readout and a slider for the follow lag |
| Banked flight (roll) | `FlightScreen` | A spaceship banking into its turns: `roll` is derived from the yaw rate and smoothed, with the ground and sky behind it. The **roll** slider scales that bank (0 = level flight, 2.5 = banks hard into every turn) and the lag slider tunes the chase |

The chase demo prints `scene.stats` (`drawn` / `culled` / `behindCamera` /
`tooSmall`) so you can see culling react as the car drives away from the camera.
Sliding `yawRate` to `0` freezes the camera yaw and makes the car appear to spin
in place — that is the lag doing its job.

The track is a stadium rather than a continuously-curving loop on purpose: on
the straights the camera's yaw stays constant, so the ground grid and the
scenery glide past in a straight line the way roadside objects do, and the
sweep that follows every turn is confined to the turns. A loop that never
straightens out keeps the camera rotating for the whole lap, which makes static
props read as if they were drifting.

The `example/sprites/torch/` folder still ships an 8-frame atlas generator
(`make_frames.py`) as a minimal frame-atlas reference.

Each sprite folder also contains the source `views.png`, a `check.png` contact
sheet, a standalone `viewer.html` and a `turntable.gif` preview.

## F1 game (Bonfire)

The **Play F1 game** button in the app bar opens `example/lib/f1_game/`, a small
[Bonfire](https://docs.page/rafaelbarbosatec/bonfire) game that drives the
Formula 1 car around a circuit.

- `W/A/S/D` or arrow keys to drive, `Space` for the handbrake. On touch devices (or via the
  touch icon next to the back button) on-screen controls appear: left/right
  steering buttons on the left, brake and accelerator pedals on the right, all
  usable at the same time with multi-touch.
- The car, trees and houses are sprite stacks. `CachedStack` pre-renders them
  into one `Picture` per yaw bucket, so the software rasterizer never runs per
  frame; Bonfire Y-sorts them like any other `GameComponent`.
- Exhaust smoke is a Flame `ParticleSystemComponent` emitted while the car moves.
- Barriers collide, grass slows you down, and the HUD tracks speed and lap times.
- The circuit is generated at runtime by `TrackTileset`: every tile is a
  `PixelArt` string packed into a `PixelTileset` atlas, so the map needs no
  image asset. Kerbs are painted only at the inside of the four corners, in a
  48 px band (three 16 px checker blocks) instead of a ribbon around the whole
  circuit. Cells crossed by the kerb boundary or by the curved asphalt/grass
  edge are rasterised pixel by pixel from the track geometry, so the red/white
  band and the green edge follow the curve instead of stepping in 32 px
  squares. `TrackLines` draws the white track-limit lines along both asphalt
  edges.
